Expand the Buddy chatbot with a set of emotional support tools designed to help students process feelings, reduce stress, and feel seen. These features should be warm, judgment-free, and age-appropriate.

1. **Add an Emotion Check-In Feature**:
   - Create a “How Are You Feeling?” button in Buddy’s chat interface
   - Let the student pick from emojis or icons:
     - 😊 Happy
     - 😐 Okay
     - 😞 Sad
     - 😠 Frustrated
     - 😰 Anxious
     - 😴 Tired

   - Based on the selected emotion, Buddy responds with empathy:
     - “I’m really glad you’re feeling good today!”
     - “That sounds tough. Want to talk about it or shift into something fun?”
     - “Feeling tired is totally normal. Maybe a low-energy activity today?”

2. **Add a “Feeling Tools” Button in the Chat**:
   When clicked, show a menu of emotional support actions:
   - 💨 Take a Breather (guides them through a 1-minute deep breathing)
   - 🎵 Quick Mood Lifter (suggests a song, a silly fact, or a joke)
   - 📝 Vent Journal (text box to write out what’s on their mind)
   - 🧘 Grounding Exercise (Buddy walks them through calming visualization)

   Keep these simple, fun, and not too clinical — use friendly language.

3. **Reflection Mode for Processing Emotions**:
   - After a tough feeling is shared, Buddy can ask:
     - “Want to tell me what happened?”
     - “What would help you feel a little better?”
     - “Let’s name the feeling and let it pass together.”

   - Store reflection entries in the student’s private journal

4. **Mood History (Optional)**:
   - Let students view a weekly mood tracker (e.g., emoji line graph)
   - Buddy can comment gently: “Looks like Tuesday was rough, but Friday was awesome!”

5. **Safe and Supportive Design**:
   - Avoid asking for too much detail or triggering questions
   - Always include a button like “I’m okay, let’s move on”
   - Frame Buddy as a partner, not a therapist

6. **Customization Options**:
   - Let students pick Buddy’s emotional support tone:
     - “Just listen”
     - “Cheer me up”
     - “Help me focus”
   - Save their preferred response style in Buddy’s memory

7. **Example Dialogue**:

Student: “I’m feeling really anxious today.”  
Buddy (Chill tone): “That’s okay. Even brave people get anxious. Want to breathe together or just talk it out a little?”  
→ Options: “Breathe,” “Vent,” “Distract me,” “No thanks”
