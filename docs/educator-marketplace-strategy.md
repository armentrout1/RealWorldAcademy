# Educator Marketplace Strategy

## Purpose

Real World Academy can become more than a curriculum library. It can become a modern online learning marketplace where educators, teachers, tutors, mentors, and creator-teachers build profiles, publish free learning samples, offer paid classes, and help families move students from point A to point B.

The marketplace should feel more like a trusted learning ecosystem than a generic gig platform.

## Core Idea

Students and families need real learning.

Educators and teachers need modern ways to teach, work from home, build audiences, and earn income.

Real World Academy sits between them as the platform that organizes learning, reviews public curriculum, handles trust and safety, tracks progress, and eventually processes paid offerings.

## Marketplace Inspiration

Fiverr is useful as a marketplace reference because it organizes independent sellers around profiles, categories, services, packages, reviews, and search. For Real World Academy, the translated model is:

- Seller profile becomes educator profile.
- Gig becomes class, tutoring offer, curriculum collection, or learning service.
- Portfolio becomes sample lessons, intro videos, published curriculum, reviews, and credentials.
- Categories become subjects, skills, trades, life skills, age groups, and learning goals.
- Packages become free sample, small group class, one-on-one session, full course, or curriculum bundle.
- Platform fee becomes Real World Academy's revenue share.

The important difference: Real World Academy has child safety, educational trust, credential honesty, and parent review responsibilities. It should not become an unrestricted gig marketplace.

## User Roles

### Learner or Student

Learners take lessons, join classes, complete projects, build portfolios, and earn non-accredited completion credentials.

### Parent or Family

Parents browse learning paths, choose educators, review progress, approve work, manage schedules, and decide which paid offerings fit their child.

### Teacher, Educator, Tutor, Mentor, or Creator

Educators create a profile, publish sample content, submit curriculum for review, offer live or recorded classes, sell tutoring/coaching, and build reputation over time.

### Admin or School Operator

Real World Academy admins review curriculum, approve educator profiles, enforce safety rules, manage reports, handle marketplace quality, and protect the brand promise.

## Educator Profile

An educator profile should eventually include:

- Display name.
- Photo or avatar.
- Bio.
- Teaching style.
- Subjects taught.
- Age groups served.
- Credentials or experience, if voluntarily listed.
- Location/time zone, if relevant.
- Intro video.
- Free sample lessons or videos.
- Published curriculum collections.
- Paid class offerings.
- Tutoring or coaching services.
- Parent/student reviews, when built.
- Trust level.
- Report or safety history visible to admins.

## Offering Types

### Free Sample Content

Purpose: help parents and students understand the educator's style.

Examples:

- Short video lesson.
- Sample worksheet.
- Mini activity.
- Intro class preview.
- Free curriculum collection.

### Paid Live Class

Purpose: scheduled teaching with a teacher or educator.

Examples:

- One-hour cooking class.
- Weekly math basics group.
- Entrepreneurship for teens.
- Beginner Spanish conversation.
- Budgeting workshop.
- Trade skill introduction.

### Paid Recorded Course

Purpose: self-paced instruction from an educator.

Examples:

- Learn addition facts.
- Money basics for middle school.
- Intro to carpentry measurements.
- Cooking fundamentals.
- Digital safety for teens.

### One-on-One Tutoring or Coaching

Purpose: individualized help.

Examples:

- Math tutoring.
- Reading practice.
- Portfolio review.
- Business idea coaching.
- Interview practice.

### Curriculum Bundle

Purpose: a complete learning path created by an educator.

Examples:

- Four-week cooking basics.
- Eight-week teen money skills.
- Six-week practical writing.
- Parent-led elementary math refresh.

## Revenue Model

Real World Academy can stay generous with free content while building a for-profit marketplace.

Potential revenue streams:

- Platform fee on paid live classes.
- Platform fee on paid recorded courses.
- Platform fee on tutoring or coaching sessions.
- Featured educator placements, only if clearly labeled.
- Premium family planning tools.
- Premium credential/portfolio exports.
- School/co-op group subscriptions later.

The platform fee should be visible and simple. Families should know what they pay. Educators should know what they keep.

## Trust and Safety Rules

- Paid educator offerings require approval before public listing.
- Educators cannot claim accreditation, licensing, or certification unless verified and supported.
- Public class pages must include age group, subject, format, price, schedule or access terms, parent expectations, and refund/cancellation policy when built.
- Classes involving minors should default to parent visibility and platform safety rules.
- External links, videos, downloadable materials, and live class conduct must be reportable.
- Admins need the ability to pause or archive educator offerings.

## MVP Marketplace Path

Do not start with payments first.

Start with trust and discovery:

1. Expand contributor profiles into educator profiles.
2. Add profile sections for sample videos, free lessons, subjects, and age groups.
3. Add offering schema for class/service listings with `free`, `paid_placeholder`, `pending_review`, `approved`, and `archived` statuses.
4. Add admin review for educator offerings.
5. Add public educator profile pages.
6. Add inquiry or interest buttons before payment processing.
7. Add payments only after the offer/review/trust model is stable.

## Product Principle

Real World Academy should help humans teach humans.

Technology should organize, review, track, and connect. It should not turn education into noise, outrage, or low-trust content.
